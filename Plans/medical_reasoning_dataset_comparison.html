Medical reasoning datasets: what they supervise, how they are built, and where Radiopaedia can add value

Recommendation: build a case-based dataset of evidence-grounded diagnostic comparisons and justified updates. Radiopaedia is a promising source for this construction, but its raw discussions are not already verified reasoning trajectories. The contribution should be the quality and structure of the supervision: which observations support which alternatives, what additional evidence changes the conclusion, and how much specificity is justified at each stage.

Existing work already provides difficult medical questions, synthetic medical chain-of-thought, step-level rewards, grounded visual explanations, expert multi-image teaching cases, and longitudinal reasoning. Therefore, “difficult medical VQA with explanations” is too broad a novelty claim. Our opportunity is to establish a useful combination of specialist-reviewed discrimination, explicit evidence availability, and transferable reasoning operations across heterogeneous radiology cases. Whether this combination improves learning remains a hypothesis.

Scope: 14 representative dataset families; released records or previews inspected for 11. This is a focused primary-source comparison, not a systematic review or a release-wide quality audit. Examples are paraphrased; key names preserve observed spelling. We did not run Lingshu, download external image collections, or clinically adjudicate images. Local counts reuse the existing Radiopaedia audit. Repository revisions, fields, and inspection limits are recorded in release_inspections.json.

1. Compare supervision units, not headline dataset sizes

A question, a case, a reasoning trajectory, an image, and a reasoning step are different counting units. Ten questions generated from one image do not provide ten independent clinical cases. Likewise, an answer label can establish whether the model chose the intended option without establishing whether its reasoning was correct.

2. Released formats, examples, and construction methods

These profiles distinguish observed release fields from paper-described methods. A field being present does not authorize passing it to the model: captions, discussions, reports, and final diagnoses often contain privileged information.

VQA-RAD paper / documentation inspected

Its original archive uses JSON, XML, and Excel with image references, QA, identifiers, and categorization. The paper distinguishes free-form questions, paraphrases, and framed variants. Clinician-generated questions mainly request short visual answers; this is not stepwise reasoning supervision. Example task type: identify an anatomical structure or the presence of a finding. We did not retrieve the original archive, so this is a documented conceptual format, not a verified raw-key list. The gap between 3,515 questions and 2,248 stored elements reflects the variants rather than contradictory image counts. Original paper, Data Records and Technical Validation.

Construction lesson: group rephrasings and repeated questions from the same image when splitting. Natural clinician questions are valuable even without long rationales; annotation granularity should match the intended skill.

SLAKE one test record inspected

img_id, img_name, question, answer, q_lang, location, modality,
answer_type, base_type, content_type, triple, qid

The observed record asks for imaging modality and answers CT. Other annotations connect visual content and medical knowledge; the release includes an image archive and a knowledge-graph archive. The paper describes bilingual QA and physician-provided semantic annotations, including spatial annotation. These are useful foundations but do not themselves constitute a validated multi-step diagnostic justification. The official site notes cleaned-release differences, so its paper count should not be treated as the current number of English training examples. Official dataset; inspected release.

Lesson: annotate whether a question depends on the image, general medical knowledge, or both. Do not label a knowledge-only question as visual reasoning merely because an image accompanies it.

PMC-VQA two raw CSV records inspected

train.csv:   Figure_path, Question, Answer, Choice A–D, Answer_label
train_2.csv: index, Figure_path, Caption, Question, Choice A–D, Answer, split

Biomedical figure captions seed automatic QA construction. In the inspected first file, Answer is answer text and Answer_label is a letter; in the second, Answer itself is a letter. One observed example concerns a breast uptake pattern; another asks which color marks an organelle. The default viewer fails because the CSV schemas differ. This is a loader issue requiring explicit file configuration, not evidence that the entire dataset is unusable. Dataset paper; inspected release.

Lesson: source captions are useful annotation material, but their facts may extend beyond what the pixels reveal. Verify that each target is answerable from the declared input, and normalize label semantics before merging files.

ReasonMed one train preview record inspected

{"instruction": "question and options", "input": "", "output": "generated response"}

The construction samples multiple solutions from several models, verifies them, and treats questions differently according to error frequency. It combines selected or refined reasoning with concise summaries. The repository contains CoTMed, ReasonMed, and ResponseMed renditions; the default preview aggregates 1,111,555 rows. These are not 1.11 million independent questions. An inspected example is a clinical toxicology vignette with a long generated rationale. The observed export has no separate source-question ID field. Paper; release and construction card.

Lesson: retain source IDs and generator/verifier provenance. Difficulty-based selection is reusable, but final-answer correctness and model agreement do not independently validate every medical premise.

MedReason one train record inspected

dataset_name, id_in_dataset, question, answer, reasoning, options
// options is a string in the observed release, not a JSON option dictionary.

The observed anatomy question has entity paths followed by prose reasoning. The method extracts question/answer entities, retrieves and prunes PrimeKG paths, and uses GPT-4o to construct explanations. Answer-based verification filters generated records; physicians from seven specialties evaluate sampled data, rather than authoring every trace. This is the UCSC 2025 MedReason dataset, distinct from similarly named later challenges. Paper; release.

Lesson: Radiopaedia articles could provide a versioned knowledge scaffold, but an article link is not a verified reasoning edge. A general association must be checked for applicability to the particular case and available findings.

Med-PRM one question with nested solutions inspected

             prm_soft_label, prm_hard_label, prm_gemini_label, prm_llama_label, …

A medication-selection vignette has ten candidate solutions in the inspected row. Step labels and retrieved documents accompany the solutions. The method trains a process reward model using retrieval-assisted judging; the release also preserves alternative labeling schemes. Soft rollout labels and model judgments are not calibrated clinical probabilities. Incorrect solutions belong in verifier training with their labels; they should not be silently flattened into positive SFT targets. Paper; release.

Lesson: store candidate trajectories separately from accepted justifications. Expert corrections to an erroneous inference may be more informative than another polished answer-correct rationale.

MedXpertQA one MM test record inspected

id, question, options, label, images,
medical_task, body_system, question_type

The observed example combines an elbow-injury vignette with radiographs and asks about operative sequencing. It includes the correct option but no rationale field. The benchmark targets challenging medical knowledge and reasoning across specialties, using expert curation. The repository provides MM and Text configurations with dev/test splits; it is not a training release. Paper; release.

Lesson: difficulty and process supervision are independent axes. A difficult test set is useful for evaluation, but does not become reliable reasoning training data by generating explanations for its held-out answers.

Eurorad cases provide expert source material, per-image findings, integrated summaries, and teaching discussions. Test distractors are selected from source differentials; leakage, text-only solvability, and surface biases are filtered. Training rows with incomplete options receive model-generated additions and revised discussions. Thus training discussions are not uniformly untouched clinician text. Paper, §2 and Appendix B.

The inspected case concerns skeletal abnormalities across six images and a syndrome diagnosis. The QA input is history plus images; the released findings, captions, discussion, and answer are privileged annotations unless a separate assisted condition is specified. The preview uses numbered image slots through 49, many null. Release.

Lesson: this is the closest direct comparator. Radiopaedia cannot claim novelty merely from expert case discussions, multi-image diagnosis, or intermediate findings. Preserve raw expert text separately from model-edited text.

GEMeX one raw open-ended record inspected

question, type, answer, reason, visual_regions, visual_locations,
image_path, ori_report, q_id, row_id

GEMeX re-grounds Chest ImaGenome report content to anatomical regions and generates four QA formats. The observed record packages a CXR question with an answer, explanation, region name, coordinates, and original report. These anatomical boxes should not automatically be interpreted as lesion contours. The annotation files were accessible; underlying MIMIC images require their own access arrangements. Paper; release.

Lesson: explicit grounding is valuable, but we should distinguish “the relevant organ is here” from “this particular abnormality is visible here.” The source report belongs in annotation provenance, not automatically in an image-only model prompt.

GEMeX-ThinkVG one train record inspected

image_path, question, thinkVG, response, question_type
// response serializes answer and region references in XML-like tags.

The inspected example reasons about bilateral lung findings with a region box. The retrieved paper is titled GEMeX-ThinkVG; the same arXiv identifier has also been indexed under GEMeX-RMCoT. A text-only DeepSeek-R1 generates traces from grounded reports and known answers; self-checking, additional model reviewers, and post-processing filter them. A radiologist samples 800 outputs for validation. This is report-mediated generation, not direct image perception by the generating model. Paper; release.

Lesson: describe such traces as reconstructed justifications. The paper’s 206,071 instances and inspected preview’s 202,384 rows differ; the cause was not established. Record both instead of silently substituting one.

Rad-ReStruct raw report and QA files inspected

QA entry: [question, answers[], history[],
           {answer_type, options[], path}]
Report: area → sub_areas → signs / diseases / objects → infos, instances

The dataset transforms IU-Xray’s expert-coded findings into a hierarchical report template and dependent QA. The inspected test record asks whether an anatomical region contains findings; subordinate attributes become relevant when findings exist. Its construction uses structured parsing of source annotations, rather than free-form CoT generation. Paper; inspected release.

Lesson: explicit prerequisite relationships make supervision and error localization easier. A hierarchical finding questionnaire is useful structure, but should not be described as a verified causal explanation or an observed clinical timeline.

ChestX-Reasoner / RadRBench-CXR paper and repository documentation inspected

The cited version uses GPT-4o to plan reasoning, extract evidence from reports, and organize the resulting steps. It combines report-based filtering with human verification of test reasoning. Its methodological format is image(s), question, answer, and reasoning steps; a complete raw training schema was not verified here. The method explicitly assumes that unmentioned findings can be treated as normal/absent. Versioned paper; published article; repository.

Lesson: do not inherit the omission-as-normal assumption for Radiopaedia. Short teaching discussions often select only educational points. Store “not mentioned,” “not visible,” “not assessed,” and “explicitly absent” separately.

RadThinking one patient JSON and repository file list inspected

Observed patient file: patient_id, total_scans, date_range, scans[]
scans[]: scan_date, age, contrast_phase, vqa, …
vqa: phase0_visual_context, phase1_segment_screening,
     phase2_findings_characterization, …

The paper describes atomic, single-step, and compositional CT questions with clinical-standard-based reasoning and longitudinal comparisons. Its appendix’s generic trace schema differs from the inspected patient file. The repository file list exposes one patient directory with 26 scans and a separate RadThinking.json; the latter and the CT pixels were not downloaded. This does not verify public availability of the entire paper cohort. Paper; release snapshot.

Lesson: separate real chronology from pedagogical information ordering. Native volumes and measurement-linked questions are strengths that rendered Radiopaedia images do not automatically provide.

Med-CMR paper and repository inspected; raw schema unverified

Clinical publications supply images and captions. Medically trained annotators design question templates; a model generates questions, multiple models propose distractors, and humans select them. Seven task categories span perceptual detail, spatial relations, temporal prediction, causal reasoning, long-tail conditions, and multi-source integration. The paper describes MCQ and open-ended evaluation. The inspected GitHub tree contained documentation and figures, so no exact released record keys are claimed. Paper; CVPR paper; repository.

Lesson: label the kind of difficulty, not simply “hard.” A task called causal reasoning is not, by that name alone, evidence of causal identification from the underlying data.

3. What the construction literature teaches us

Design principleWhy it mattersImplication for Radiopaedia
Separate outcome truth from the conclusion warranted by available evidenceA later confirmed label can be correct while an earlier uniquely certain diagnosis is unjustified.Store final outcome and stage-specific warranted conclusion as separate targets. Allow a differential or partial conclusion.
Separate source evidence, generated explanation, and accepted annotationPolishing an author’s text changes its provenance; a generated trace can repeat a correct answer for the wrong reason.Retain raw text, generation history, reviewer decisions, and edits. Never overwrite source text with synthetic prose.
Verify answerability from the exact model inputA caption or discussion may mention an unseen sequence, pathology, follow-up, or an unshown image.Review the selected image set and clinical history together. “Available in the source case” is weaker than “present in this training input.”
Annotate propositions and their supportWhole-trace approval hides whether an error is visual, factual, inferential, or due to missing evidence.Attach image or text evidence references, polarity, inference premises, alternatives, and applicability conditions to important steps.
Make difficulty conditional and multidimensionalRarity, obscurity, bad images, and reasoning complexity are different sources of low accuracy.Record perception, knowledge, discrimination, integration, uncertainty, and artifact labels separately. Preserve easy controls.
Use clinically plausible alternativesRandom distractors mostly test elimination of obviously unrelated choices.Draft from the source differential, then obtain specialist review. Keep open-ended targets alongside MCQs and monitor option-length cues.
Split the source before expanding itParaphrases, frames, staged reveals, and synthetic trajectories share information.Assign a case/duplicate group to one split before generating questions. Keep all sibling records together.
Evaluate independent grounding, not only agreement with source proseA report-grounded judge can reproduce omissions or incorrect alignments in that report.Begin with image review blinded to the diagnosis, followed by source review. Track human disagreement rather than forcing agreement.

These are our design recommendations drawn from the comparison, not guarantees supplied by any single dataset. In particular, a chain-of-thought string is a serialization choice. The scientifically meaningful unit is a checkable observation or inference under a declared evidence set. A short comparison can provide stronger supervision than a long retrospective narrative.

The main extra work is not asking an LLM to expand discussions. 1. Source caseImmutable text, assets, authorship, versions
2. Evidence statesExactly what the model may observe

Use a normalized case record as the source of truth. Generate chat-format SFT, preference pairs, or process-reward examples from this record afterward. A single flat {image, question, answer, cot} file loses the distinctions our research needs.

What “expert-level reasoning data” would mean operationally

Use a specialist to validate the decisive visual observations and inference transitions within the relevant domain. A second reviewer independently checks the task before seeing the first reviewer’s conclusion; unresolved disagreements remain recorded. For example, head-and-neck expertise should verify salivary-lesion comparisons, and neuroradiology expertise should verify brain-lesion comparisons. Source-author identity alone does not satisfy this protocol.

The target is a compact, clinically defensible justification: key observations → credible alternatives → evidence that changes their relative plausibility → warranted conclusion → unresolved information. Preserve multiple acceptable justifications when appropriate. Call the resulting data expert-reviewed; “expert-level model reasoning” requires a subsequent performance comparison with experts under matched input conditions.

How the same construction could support meta-learning

Tag each task along two independent axes: clinical family and reasoning operation. A family might be salivary masses or intracranial ring-enhancing lesions; an operation might be integrating complementary sequences, recognizing an exception to a usual rule, or revising a diagnosis after new evidence. These tags should be specialist-reviewed and multi-label when needed.

An adaptation episode would contain support cases and different query cases from a declared family, with all patient/duplicate groups disjoint. Reserve some families for transfer evaluation. If support and query share only rewritten questions about the same case, the task measures memorization. If the “experts” differ only by disease-name prompts, the data do not establish specialist expertise. Preserve this episode structure now; algorithmic meta-learning can wait until the labels and baseline comparisons are credible.

6. A practical construction and quality-control workflow

  1. Freeze and inventory the source. Record snapshot hashes, case/article links, original wording, asset counts, and extraction versions. Keep overlapping _new metadata out of counts of new cases. Maintain attribution and access/release metadata; this review does not establish redistribution rights for every asset.
  2. Create candidate pools with two sampling tracks. A random track estimates source yield and quality. An enriched track searches for explicit differential comparisons, confirmation, uncertainty, or complementary sequences. Do not use the enriched track to estimate the prevalence of hard cases.
  3. Audit image packages before writing reasoning targets. Resolve duplicate frames, sequence labels, repeated viewer text, image–case mismatch, visible answer overlays, and missing referenced sequences. Record whether expert review used the complete source series or the exact reduced model input. A diagnosis established from the complete series may not be supportable from selected frames.
  4. Make the first clinical read without the answer. Provide the intended history and images, withholding title, diagnosis-bearing links, source discussion, and confirmation. Ask for findings, alternatives, specificity, and missing information. This reduces retrospective rationalization during annotation.
  5. Reconcile with source material in a second pass. Reveal the discussion and outcome; annotate which facts came from images, history, general knowledge, later tests, or author assertion. Repair the proposed question if its answer requires unprovided evidence. Never turn a general statement about pathology into confirmation for that patient.
  6. Construct a small set of tasks per case. Prioritize differential discrimination, complementary-evidence integration, rule applicability, and justified updates. Include straightforward finding questions as controls and insufficiency targets where justified. Use source-based alternatives with expert approval; include open-ended evaluation to reduce dependence on distractor design.
  7. Use models for drafting, then verify the high-value claims. A model may extract candidate findings, propose alternatives, or format annotations. Log its version, prompt, access to the answer, and edits. Independent experts check the decisive claims and the full held-out evaluation targets. Automated agreement is a filter, not specialist adjudication.
  8. Split before generating renditions or episodes. Combine source-case IDs, duplicate image components, likely republished cases, and known patient links. Keep articles and retrieved snippets under a declared retrieval policy; do not let a held-out case’s diagnosis-bearing page enter support examples. Record unresolved linkage instead of claiming perfect patient independence.
  9. Export distinct learning products. Answer-only and accepted-justification SFT should share identical inputs. Export labeled incorrect trajectories for process supervision or preference learning separately. Preserve source lineage across all forms, with a single case-level sampling weight to limit domination by prolific cases.
  10. Publish the construction accounting. Report candidates, exclusions by reason, accepted cases, informative image sets, questions, trajectories, reviewed steps, and expert minutes. Provide label definitions, reviewer coverage, disagreement handling, representative examples, and a locked evaluation subset. A dataset card should identify source overlap with prior public benchmarks and possible pretraining exposure.

Start with a feasibility sample, not a promise of a large reasoning corpus

The existing title-based pools contain 185 parotid cases, 442 intracranial-lesion candidates, and 253 adrenal cases. The narrower lexical intersections of confirmation and uncertainty contain only 3, 24, and 6 respectively; these are noisy search yields, not clinical eligibility counts. Local candidate-pool analysis. We should therefore inspect actual yield before choosing a final dataset size.

A reasonable planning target is a 60-case feasibility review, approximately 20 per candidate family, with a separately recorded random source sample if corpus-wide quality estimates are desired. This number is a workflow proposal, not a power calculation. Expand only after establishing that sufficient cases have visible evidence, useful comparisons, independent specialist agreement, and manageable annotation cost. Broaden families if the eligible intersection is too small.

7. Research judgment and recommended positioning

The best initial contribution is a carefully constructed supervision resource, not a larger synthetic CoT dump. Establish that a subset of Radiopaedia cases can support reliable specialist-reviewed comparisons under explicitly controlled evidence. Build a modest evaluation set with reviewed observations and acceptable conclusions, plus a training pool whose annotation depth is documented.

The closest comparator to read and reproduce conceptually is MedThinkVQA. Borrow grounding structure from GEMeX, candidate/step-label separation from Med-PRM, dependency structure from Rad-ReStruct, and explicit decomposition from RadThinking. Use ReasonMed and MedReason as construction baselines for generated or knowledge-guided rationales. These comparisons make the proposed contribution precise rather than claiming an empty field.

Suggested research claim: “We construct specialist-reviewed radiological reasoning tasks that link diagnostic comparisons to supplied image evidence and distinguish current diagnostic warrant from retrospective confirmation. The dataset preserves evidence provenance, alternative hypotheses, and family-level structure, enabling controlled study of reasoning enhancement and transfer in pretrained medical MLLMs.”

This claim is conditional on completing the annotation and validation work. The local corpus already supplies promising cases, article links, and rich image inventories. It does not yet supply validated difficulty, per-image reasoning alignment, or expert-reviewed inference labels. Those are the work that could make the dataset valuable.
