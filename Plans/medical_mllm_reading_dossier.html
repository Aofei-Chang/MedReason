What medical MLLMs must learn
about the limits of evidence

A focused methods audit and proposed Radiopaedia study. Eleven core papers read across methods, data construction, results, and relevant appendices; two recent papers checked specifically for conceptual overlap. Published papers and preprints are distinguished below. No experiments or code reproductions were run.

Working conclusion: the central research gap is supervision and evaluation of whether the evidence actually available for a patient warrants the model’s claims—including their specificity, uncertainty, and use of clinical rules.

This is a synthesis, not a claim that every medical model has the same defect. A coherent explanation, a correct eventual diagnosis, a relevant guideline, and an image-sensitive output each supply useful evidence. None alone establishes that a particular inference was justified from the information shown. Conversely, a model can make a useful probabilistic prediction without a deterministic proof; the goal is to make the claim and its uncertainty appropriate to the evidence.

The useful research question is therefore narrower than “Can guidelines improve medical reasoning?” It is: can a modest amount of clinician-supervised data teach transferable boundaries on what can be concluded as evidence becomes available? That must be tested against strong process-supervision and evidence-agent baselines, rather than presumed novel.

1. Close-reading notes

Dr-LLaVA

2024 · arXiv v2

Read here: Main methods; Appendices A–C, especially C.4 and Table C.5.

Intuition, data, and method. Expert symbolic pathways constrain dialogue about 16,340 bone-marrow image patches. Image quality and cell adequacy precede diagnosis; inconclusive branches already exist. Template-derived conversations train the model with correctness, consistency, and anti-hacking rewards.

Evidence to inspect. Removing correctness reward raises symbolic consistency while single-turn question accuracy falls from 89.6 to 32.4; invalid-path rate falls from 22.5 to 1.5. These are percentages, not clinical outcome measures.

Interpretation and boundary. Demonstrated: pathway consistency can dissociate sharply from image-conditioned correctness. The authors explicitly discuss this trade-off. This is evidence for combining premise verification with rules, not evidence that symbolic guidance is useless.

ChestX-Reasoner

2026 · Communications Medicine

Read here: Methods: RadRScore and RadRBench-CXR; process factuality reward. Results: training-strategy ablation, Fig. 6.

Intuition, data, and method. GPT-4o constructs reasoning from QA pairs and reports: 59K reasoning-augmented training examples accompany 1.2M answer-only examples. Process reward compares generated observations with reports; radiologists also assess generated reasoning.

Evidence to inspect. The construction and factuality metric treat unmentioned findings as normal/no disease. Adding process reward changes average RadRScore from 0.512 to 0.525: 0.013 absolute, approximately 2.5% relative.

Interpretation and boundary. Design limitation: report silence substitutes for an explicit negative observation. Whether this produces errors requires an image-level audit. Retrospectively generated explanations are not recordings of clinicians’ original reasoning.

Med-PRM

2025 · EMNLP

Read here: Sections 3–4 and 7.3; Table 3; Appendix I.

Intuition, data, and method. Retrieval supplies medical references for binary step labels; an 8B process model scores text reasoning. The training pool includes 10,178 MedQA questions plus 500 each from three additional datasets, with sampled traces. Uses include best-of-N selection and rejection-sampling SFT.

Evidence to inspect. Expert alignment is measured on 180 steps from six questions. Correlations are 0.74 on the easy subset and 0.71 on the hard subset. Thus the work does validate its judge, although on a small question sample.

Interpretation and boundary. Scope boundary: verifying a textual medical inference does not independently verify an image-derived premise. This is not a multimodal PRM or evidence that a reference-informed judge transfers reliably to uncertain visual findings.

Act as You Think / MCR

2026 · ACL

Read here: Section 2.2; Section 3.4; Tables 6–8; Limitations.

Intuition, data, and method. Med-Zero-17K combines new and existing medical VQA. The reward combines answer correctness, text reasoning–answer coherence, and original-versus-corrupted-image behavior. The coherence judge receives question, reasoning, and answer; it does not independently inspect the image.

Evidence to inspect. Table 6: adding the tested reasoning-correctness judge lowers OmniMedVQA accuracy from vanilla GRPO’s 65.4 to 63.9; MCR reaches 69.2. Judge identity also differs between recipes, limiting attribution.

Interpretation and boundary. Demonstrated: this judge-reward recipe underperforms. Not demonstrated: correctness supervision is inherently inferior. Image-corruption sensitivity is useful evidence dependence, but does not establish that a particular clinical finding justifies a particular diagnosis.

MedGuideX

2026 · arXiv v1

Read here: Sections 3.3–4; Eqs. 6–7; Appendices C and F.

Intuition, data, and method. 841 guideline documents yield 2,793 executable functions and factual/counterfactual scenarios. Counterfactual construction retains changed outcomes and discards unchanged outcomes. Its reward checks the intended hidden state, factual consistency, and counterfactual output.

Evidence to inspect. The selected final recipe uses mixed factual/counterfactual SFT followed by factual GRPO. Factual reward checks the final guideline output. It should not be described as universal step-by-step clinical verification.

Interpretation and boundary. Open audit: is every hidden state uniquely recoverable from the shown information? The reviewed construction does not establish this. Executable oracle correctness and answer identifiability are separate properties. See the constructed example below; it is not a discovered dataset error.

MMedExpert-R1

2026 · arXiv v1

Read here: Sections 3.1–3.2; guideline-based adaptation, Eqs. 4–5; Table 3.

Intuition, data, and method. The raw pool is 10,000 examples, but the retained training set described in the methods is 3,900: 3,300 text and 600 multimodal. GPT-5 produces traces organized into differential, intuitive, analytical, and Bayesian reasoning paradigms, followed by filtering and professional review.

Evidence to inspect. Domain adapters, guideline-based adaptation, and merging provide complementary components. The adaptation objective broadcasts a trajectory-level advantage rather than assigning independently verified clinical-step credit.

Interpretation and boundary. Important distinction: a general reasoning paradigm is not a disease-specific practice guideline. The reviewed reward description does not establish a clinical applicability verifier. Claims that this is 10K curated multimodal reasoning data would misstate the methods.

FaithMed

2026 · arXiv v1

Read here: Sections 3–5; Table 3; Section 8, Limitations.

Intuition, data, and method. Clinician-designed evidence-based medicine rubrics guide a text search agent. Twenty-six candidate rubrics become fifteen, with action-dependent applicability and step-level advantage assignment. The dimensions cover asking, acquiring, appraising, applying, and assessing evidence.

Evidence to inspect. For the 4B no-SFT setting, Table 3 reports 56.5 for outcome reward, 56.8 for episode process reward, and 61.2 for step process reward. Credit assignment matters within this controlled comparison.

Interpretation and boundary. This directly blocks a novelty claim about generic clinical meta-guidelines or step-level evidence supervision. Its own limitations acknowledge imperfect rubrics/judges and possible unsupported claims. Visual evidence sufficiency and transfer to new imaging conditions remain outside the demonstrated text-agent setting.

MedAgent-Pro

2025 · arXiv v3

Read here: Sections 3.2–3.4; Eq. 4; Table 4; Limitations.

Intuition, data, and method. A guideline-derived plan coordinates specialist visual tools and language reasoning. Available data constrain the plan, and the controller can continue, terminate, or complete. Thus missing-data handling and diagnostic stopping already have prior implementations.

Evidence to inspect. In the glaucoma ablation, balanced accuracy moves from 56.4 at baseline to 75.9 with planning, then 88.5 after quantitative knowledge, and 90.4 with evidence-based reasoning.

Interpretation and boundary. Interpretation: a large contribution comes from obtaining useful measurements, so better reasoning cannot replace perception. The incremental components include tools and information, not only reasoning algorithms. A future comparison must hold tool access and evidence availability constant.

MedCBR

2026 · arXiv v1

Read here: Sections 3.1–3.4; formulation immediately after the classical CBM description; Table 2.

Intuition, data, and method. Breast-imaging descriptors and guideline-conditioned reports supervise a multitask visual model. Reports are generated with images, positive concept labels, diagnostic labels, and guidelines. A frozen reasoning model explains predicted concepts and diagnosis.

Evidence to inspect. The actual formulation is diagnosis = q(h), concepts = g(h), explanation = R(diagnosis, concepts, guidelines). Diagnosis and concepts share features, but the diagnostic head is not constrained to consume the predicted concept vector.

Interpretation and boundary. Architectural conclusion: intervening on the displayed concepts need not change that diagnostic head. The explanation therefore does not establish causal mediation of the prediction. This is compatible with useful concept and diagnostic performance gains.

MRPO / Breaking Failure Cascades

2026 · arXiv v1

Read here: Section 4.1, Eq. 7; Appendices B.1, D.3, and H.2.

Intuition, data, and method. 13,381 open-ended questions over 3,556 images train a step-aware GRPO variant. A judge receives the image, reference reasoning, and answer. A step is valid if gold alignment OR answer contribution passes; the prompt treats “cannot determine” as evasion.

Evidence to inspect. Extra early-step penalties apply when the final answer is wrong. Correct-answer trajectories still receive the aggregate process reward. The selective-penalty choice is supported by its ablation; 178 judged steps yield 85.5% human agreement.

Interpretation and boundary. Boundary: this task assumes answerability and progress toward the known answer. Our proposed audit concerns justified uncertainty and correct answers with false premises; neither is established by agreement with this shared rubric.

Med-OPD

2026 · arXiv v1

Read here: Sections 3.2–3.5; Eq. 4; Section 4.1; Table 1.

Intuition, data, and method. An answer-aware teacher scores student tokens with the original image and an image downsampled to 10% resolution then enlarged. Positive likelihood differences weight tokens and trajectories during on-policy distillation. Four OmniMedVQA subsets each use 1,000 training and 300 test examples.

Evidence to inspect. Average answer accuracy rises from standard OPD’s 0.6775 to 0.7058. Reported quantitative evaluation is answer-option accuracy, complemented by a qualitative example.

Interpretation and boundary. Design limitation: teacher sensitivity to broad degradation is a proxy for clinically necessary evidence. It does not establish localized causal support or proper uncertainty after evidence loss. We cannot claim novelty merely from counterfactual image-conditioned token weighting.

MedTRACE

2026 · arXiv v1 · targeted overlap check

Read here: Sections 3.1–3.5 and 4.1–4.5; Eq. 8; metric definitions.

Intuition, data, and method. The agent explicitly models evidence sufficiency, tool acquisition, evidence memory, support/contradiction/insufficiency, and confidence. Training includes diagnostic, tool, evidence, verification, grounding, and calibration objectives on image/text/EHR/ECG settings.

Evidence to inspect. This is a very close conceptual predecessor. Its unsupported-diagnostic-error metric requires both an incorrect diagnosis and evidence that fails to match expert support.

Interpretation and boundary. Consequently, that metric does not count a correct diagnosis supported by an invalid premise. A separately adjudicated false-premise/correct-answer evaluation would test a different failure. Novelty cannot rest on adding an evidence ledger, sufficiency score, or stop/review action alone.

Spatial and factual RL for CXR reports

2026 · arXiv v1 · targeted overlap check

Read here: Enhancing Visual Reasoning in Chest X-Ray Report Generation Using Reinforcement Learning. Method, Eq. 5; Tables 4–6; Limitations.

Intuition, data, and method. Chest ImaGenome regions, bounding boxes, and findings supply structured reasoning. A spatial-overlap gate determines whether regional factual reward is counted; the threshold is 0.25. The model also receives final-report supervision.

Evidence to inspect. Table 6’s 25%-data RL ablation reports RadGraph-F1 of 20.27 with factual-only reward, 21.18 without either reward, and 23.73 with both. The authors describe spatial-only duplication and incorrect region–text association.

Interpretation and boundary. This blocks novelty claims for gated spatial/factual process rewards. It demonstrates complementary supervision, while reference-report agreement and anatomical overlap still do not establish every inferential claim’s necessity or sufficiency.

2. What follows from the reading

The problem has three separable parts. First, ascertain the patient facts: what is visible, what is explicitly absent, and what cannot be assessed? Second, apply knowledge only within its scope: are the prerequisites and exceptions of the clinical rule satisfied? Third, decide what claim is warranted: a finding, a differential, a probability, a specific diagnosis, or a request for additional evidence. A fluent narrative can conceal failure in any one of these parts.

Process supervision has two different meanings. Scoring the content of a reasoning trace and assigning learning credit to particular steps are different operations. Both can help, but neither makes the scorer correct. Before choosing an RL algorithm, establish whether the verifier rejects a persuasive false premise, accepts a valid alternative path, and recognizes genuinely insufficient evidence. Otherwise more precise credit assignment can amplify a mistaken judgment.

Guideline transfer is conditional, not analogical by appearance. Similar visual findings do not establish the same disease, clinical population, or management indication. The transferable object should include its task, prerequisites, exclusions, uncertain inputs, and allowed conclusion. General reasoning procedures can transfer; disease thresholds and recommendations require their own validated scope. Diagnostic evidence, risk assessment, and management recommendations must remain distinct.

“Small high-quality data beats scale” remains a hypothesis. Few carefully annotated cases could expose supervision that large answer-only datasets lack. They cannot establish coverage of every visual manifestation, acquisition protocol, or disease. Use an already competent visual backbone and compare annotation efficiency. Any result should specify which behavior improved per clinician-hour, at what cost to diagnostic performance.

Claims we should avoid: “Nobody uses guidelines,” “nobody models uncertainty,” “there are no process benchmarks,” and “counterfactual grounding is new.” The papers above already invalidate these broad framings. The defensible contribution would be a better-defined supervision target, independently adjudicated evaluation, and demonstrated transfer.

3. Radiopaedia: supervise information states, not just case labels

I have not inspected your extracted dataset. Public indexed case material only establishes feasibility examples, not the prevalence or quality of fields in your collection. Direct retrieval of the selected case pages was unavailable, so the following observation is explicitly based on indexed Radiopaedia material.

Radiopaedia case rID 198678 identifies excision histology as the definitive confirmation of acinic cell carcinoma. That is a concrete reason to preserve the source of diagnostic certainty separately from the images. It does not, by itself, prove which differential an image-only reader should give; blinded clinician annotation must establish that.

The proposed data unit is a case with several controlled information states. Each state has its own permissible conclusions. If actual chronology is unavailable, call these information states, not the patient’s reconstructed clinical timeline. Do not manufacture intermediate doctor decisions from the final discussion.

Annotation procedure. First, clinicians review only the permitted input, without the title, final diagnosis, or discussion. They annotate findings and what conclusions are supportable. Next, reveal the confirmation and ask which earlier uncertainty it resolves. Retain disagreement instead of forcing false unanimity. An adjudicator should distinguish a genuinely ambiguous case from an annotation error. Remove diagnosis-bearing filenames, overlays, embedded captions, and discussion leakage from model inputs.

Adapt a hard case by changing available information, not by inventing pathology. Where the source supports it, derive: image-only; image plus existing clinical context; additional original series; and confirmatory evidence. Pair removal of decisive evidence with removal of irrelevant evidence. Add matched cases with similar appearance but different confirmed diagnoses, and cases with the same diagnosis but different manifestations. These variants share one base case and must stay in the same split.

Withholding an original sequence is an evidence intervention. Masking a lesion is a stress test with possible artifacts. Generating a different-looking image is not automatically a clinically valid disease counterfactual. Use real information reveals and clinician-validated case contrasts for the primary experiment; image synthesis can be a separate, independently checked extension.

Case reuse should transfer a local inference pattern together with its scope. A useful pattern might be “these findings support a family of explanations, but this discriminating feature is unobserved.” It should not become “a similar image implies the same final diagnosis.” Audit confirmation type, study completeness, duplicates, and permissible reuse before estimating how many usable cases exist.

4. A reasoning design worth testing

Represent the output as a compact, inspectable claim record: observations with evidence pointers; candidate explanations and contradictions; applicable rules with satisfied and unknown prerequisites; and a conclusion at the supported level of specificity. The target is a verifiable clinical artifact, not a purported transcript of the model’s hidden cognition.

claim validity = evidence support + applicable inference + appropriate specificity
success = valid claims AND useful performance on answerable cases

The expression is a design decomposition, not an established numerical reward. Initially keep these judgments separate. An average score can hide a critical unsupported premise behind several easy correct statements. For a conclusion that explicitly depends on a premise, test a gate or minimum-support criterion against an additive score. Such gates are not themselves new; the experiment must show that the clinical annotations make them more reliable.

A practical meta-guideline is a procedure for using knowledge under partial observation:

  1. Establish which patient facts are actually observed and which are missing or unreliable.
  2. Retrieve or select a rule whose task and patient scope match; expose unknown prerequisites.
  3. Compare supported alternatives and identify evidence that could discriminate among them.
  4. State a conclusion with warranted specificity; preserve uncertainty where the available information does not distinguish alternatives.
  5. Revise when relevant evidence changes; remain stable when an irrelevant detail changes.

This procedure must be instantiated with clinical annotations. It is not a universal guideline that authorizes inferring disease-specific thresholds. Generalization is demonstrated only if the model transfers the procedure to held-out combinations, conditions, or rule families without importing invalid conclusions.

Train the verifier before trusting it. Create minimally different candidate outputs: a correct conclusion with a fabricated decisive finding; valid uncertainty versus premature specificity; the right guideline applied outside its scope; and a correct alternative explanation absent from the reference trace. Ask independent clinicians to label support and permissible inference. Blind them to the producing model and training reward. Report inter-rater disagreement and critical false-accept rates.

Begin model training with supervised claim records and targeted preference pairs. Compare that to answer-only SFT, explanation SFT, and outcome RL. Introduce a learned process reward only if its independent evaluation is good enough to justify optimization. Keep image support, rule applicability, and sufficiency judgments observable in the logs. Do not train every partially observed case to output its eventual confirmed diagnosis with certainty.

Constructed identifiability test—not a reported MedGuideX bug. Let a toy rule be Y = A OR B. We observe A = 1 and Y = 1, while B is hidden. Both B = 0 and B = 1 fit. After setting A to 0, the outcome depends on the unknown B. An executable rule can provide a unique answer for the creator’s complete state while the learner’s shown state admits two answers. The correct audit checks uniqueness or scores a set of possible outcomes; it does not penalize a defensible “insufficient information.” Clinical counterfactual datasets should explicitly distinguish these situations.

5. The experiment that would make the claim credible

Start with a feasibility pilot of roughly 200–300 base cases in one or two coherent imaging tasks. This is a proposed scope, not a sample-size justification. Select tasks after auditing available series, confirmation, ambiguity, and clinician access. Reserve an untouched case-level test set before constructing variants. Use the pilot to estimate effect sizes, disagreement, and clustering for a powered follow-up.

Primary endpoint: the proportion of cases with an unsupported clinically consequential claim, adjudicated against the information actually shown. Count these even when the final diagnosis happens to be correct. Report the endpoint at matched answerable-case coverage, alongside the risk–coverage curve, so indiscriminate abstention cannot win.

Companion endpoints: visual-premise accuracy; confusion between absent and unassessable findings; over-specific conclusions; retention of valid alternatives; appropriate belief revision; invariance to irrelevant changes; and time required for clinician review. If probabilities are collected, use a proper scoring rule and calibration measures against a clearly defined target. Do not treat verbal confidence or one aggregate metric as sufficient.

Critical hard-negative test. Supply two explanations ending in the same correct diagnosis: one grounded in the actual image, one relying on an invented finding. The verifier must prefer the grounded one. Then test a supportable differential against an unjustifiably certain correct label before confirmatory evidence is revealed. This directly separates correctness of the eventual label from justification of the current claim.

Baselines. Include guideline retrieval, an expert pathway approach, report-derived process supervision, spatial/factual supervision, and an evidence-sufficiency agent where implementable. Use the same backbone, tools, retrieval corpus, and evidence budget for causal comparisons. A leaderboard model comparison is useful context but does not isolate the contribution. Compare clinician annotation minutes as well as example count and GPU cost.

Splits and contamination. Group all views, follow-ups, augmentations, and related case material before splitting; group patients when identifiers permit. Deduplicate image and text neighbors. Public-case memorization by pretrained models remains a limitation even after careful downstream splitting. A new institutional holdout is needed before claims about clinical generalization. Hard educational cases also cannot estimate routine-care prevalence, calibration, or clinical net benefit without appropriate additional sampling.

Analysis. Predefine the primary contrast and severity rubric. Bootstrap at the base-case level, not the derived-QA level; record clinician and case clustering. Report both the aggregate effect and failure categories. An expert reader study can test whether the evidence record helps clinicians notice and correct model errors, but a retrospective study does not establish patient benefit or actual adoption.

Conditions that would weaken or kill the idea: clinicians cannot reliably annotate the inference boundary; available cases lack meaningful information contrasts; the model merely learns to abstain; gains exist only under the training judge; a matched evidence-agent baseline already solves the proposed failures; or improvement disappears on unseen case families. Each outcome should change the project, not be hidden by a larger answer-accuracy table.

6. What remains uncertain—and what to read first

The strongest established findings concern specific reward failures and specific architectural or data assumptions. The larger claim—that evidence-state supervision will improve transferable clinical reliability—is still a research hypothesis. This review does not show that data scale has stopped helping, that a small dataset can build a competent visual encoder, or that doctors will adopt a system with longer explanations.

For your own reading, start with Dr-LLaVA C.4–C.5, ChestX-Reasoner’s data/reward definitions, MedCBR’s actual prediction graph, and MedGuideX’s construction and reward equations. Then read FaithMed, MRPO, MedTRACE, and the spatial/factual CXR paper to constrain the novelty claim. Med-PRM is particularly useful for studying how to validate a process judge.

The first concrete output should be an adjudicated evidence-boundary benchmark and a verifier audit, followed by a training comparison. If those expose a reproducible failure that existing guideline and process methods do not resolve, the method contribution has a clear target. If they do not, we should revise the hypothesis before scaling data collection.
